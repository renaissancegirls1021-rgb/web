This is the Kramskoi piece!

The Unknown Woman

One of Kramskoi's most famous works. Its original title means “An Unknown Woman,” while the Japanese title, The Stranger, was given when the painting was brought to Japan. The woman's gaze as she rides through a snow-covered city exudes an indescribable elegance. Initially, however, the painting was poorly received and even condemned as a depiction of a prostitute. Over time, it gained recognition and is now one of the most celebrated portraits in Russian art.

Some say this woman is Anna, the heroine of Anna Karenina, the novel published shortly before this painting by our great national writer, Tolstoy. But no one really knows!

Who is Kramskoi?

Ivan Kramskoi

A leading artist of the Russian Peredvizhniki (Wanderers). As a student, he rebelled against the Academy of Arts' conservative policies and was expelled. He later became a key member of the group he founded with fellow artists, traveling across Russia and becoming a renowned portraitist. He painted many prominent contemporaries, including Tolstoy, Shishkin, and Tretyakov. His portraits capture not only their expressions and gazes but also their thoughtful inner lives.
