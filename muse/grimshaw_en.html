This is the Grimshaw piece!

Moonlit night, near Leeds

Leeds is a city in northern England, the town where Grimshaw was born and raised. Whether it had just rained or not, a full moon illuminated the bluish sky, its light reflecting off puddles on the muddy ground. Grimshaw painted many night scenes of real towns, but his unique palette—slightly muted, blue-green hues—possesses a poetic, fantastical quality, blending beauty with a touch of melancholy.

Who is Grimshaw?

John Atkinson Grimshaw

British painter of the Victorian era. Originally employed as a railway worker, he pursued painting against his parents' wishes. He favored depicting damp-textured British nightscapes—street lamps and cobblestones floating in bluish mist—whose fantastical light evokes the influence of Britain's own Pre-Raphaelite movement. He left behind no diaries or letters, and his name remains relatively unknown in Japan. However, his works, imbued with a romantic melancholy, have been gaining renewed attention in recent years.
