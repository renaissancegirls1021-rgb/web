This is the Millais piece!

Ophelia

This painting depicts a woman who appears in Shakespeare's play "Hamlet." When Ophelia learns that Hamlet, the man she loves, has murdered her father, she goes mad with grief and drowns in the river while singing. This is a masterpiece that beautifully depicts the transience and beauty of a woman who meets a tragic death, with meticulous depictions of nature typical of the Pre-Raphaelites.

I painted the plants near the River Hogsmill close to London, and the local pastor actually recorded exactly where I was painting. So diligent—it’s kind of hilarious!

Who is Millais?

Sir John Everett Millais

He was a genius who entered the British Academy at the youngest age in history and was one of the central figures in the formation of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood. His paintings, which were based on meticulous observation of their subjects, still possess a timeless beauty. In his youth, he was a rebellious painter, but in his later years he softened his attitude and shifted to a style that appealed to the masses, achieving success and rising to the position of Director of the Academy. However, at the same time, the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, whose motto was rebellion against authority, also came to an end.
