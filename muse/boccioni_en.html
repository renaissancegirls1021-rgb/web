This is the Boccioni piece!

Unique Forms of Continuity in Space

This sculpture captures the dynamic act of walking through a stationary figure. Its streamlined form brilliantly expresses the Futurists’ obsession with objects in motion. Originally made in plaster, several bronze casts were produced after Boccioni’s death. The work remains widely admired and even appears on Italian euro coins.

This sculpture even became the winner’s trophy at the Gran Turismo World Series! The glory of Futurism—and me—is eternal! Gya-ha-ha-ha!

Who is Boccioni?

Umberto Boccioni

One of the leading artists of Italian Futurism. He began his career as a commercial illustrator but joined the movement after poet Marinetti published the Futurist Manifesto. Through painting and sculpture, he embodied Futurism’s celebration of speed, machines, energy, and military power. He enlisted in the army after World War I broke out, but died at 33 following a fall from a horse during training. Despite his short life, he left behind many works that came to define Futurism.
