This is the Magritte piece!

The Empire of Light

This is a landscape painting depicting a townscape, yet the nighttime city and daytime sky coexist simultaneously, separated only by the street trees. Both day and night should be familiar sights to us, yet their juxtaposition within a single frame creates a strange space. This employs “dépaysement”—a technique favored by Surrealists that combines elements that would not normally meet. Magritte, in particular, is known for his frequent use of this technique. It is also said that the British painter Grimshaw served as a reference for the creation of this work.

This subject somehow really captured my heart. I changed the size and materials, and ended up painting twenty-seven pieces in total.

Who is Magritte?

René Magritte

A painter from Belgium. In his early years, he painted Cubist-style portraits, but he was profoundly moved by the works of De Chirico, who was influenced by Dadaism, and gradually became devoted to Surrealist painting. His signature style featured trompe l'oeil-like works that subverted the imagery associated with words and motifs. Often described as enigmatic, this approach significantly shaped the impression of the Surrealist movement as a whole. He was also known for setting up his easel in his kitchen and painting while wearing a suit.
