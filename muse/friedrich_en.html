This is the Friedrich piece!

Wanderer above the Sea of Fog

A lone man stands atop a rocky summit, quietly gazing over a vast sea of clouds below. His back suggests both the strength of someone who has overcome a difficult journey and a deep sense of solitude, making it easy to see the artist himself reflected in the figure. A solitary thinker contemplating nature on such a grand scale is a quintessential Romantic motif. Although the painting is often translated into Japanese as "Traveler Above the Sea of Clouds", the original title is closer in meaning to Wanderer or Wayfarer.

Pretty sharp-looking coat, isn't it? A historian named Arndt, whom I met back then, recommended it to me. It's meant to symbolize the spirit of the German people.

Who is Friedrich?

Caspar David Friedrich

A leading painter of German Romanticism. Born in Greifswald, then part of Swedish Pomerania, he is now regarded as a German artist. He became famous for misty mountains and desolate seascapes filled with quiet melancholy. As a child, he lost his younger brother in an accident while the boy was trying to save him, a tragedy that deeply influenced his art. Though his popularity faded in later years, he was rediscovered in the 20th century and is now regarded as a master of German Romanticism.
