This is the Gericault piece!

The Raft of the Medusa

In the early nineteenth century, the French frigate Méduse, bound for the colony of Senegal, ran aground, leading to a tragic disaster that claimed the lives of most of its roughly 150 passengers and crew. Determined to turn the event into a masterpiece, Géricault conducted extensive research, including interviews with survivors. By capturing both the horror of the tragedy and a faint glimmer of hope, the painting became a landmark that helped launch French Romanticism.

To paint true despair, I even borrowed corpses from the morgue and sketched them as they decomposed! You can't paint the real thing unless you know what it's really like!

Who is Gericault?

Théodore Géricault

A pioneering French Romantic painter, Géricault preferred real-life events over biblical or mythological subjects, creating powerful works based on contemporary incidents and portraits. He also had a lifelong passion for horses, painting many racehorses and cavalry scenes. His dynamic style anticipated later developments in Impressionism. Though he died in his thirties from complications following a riding accident, both his art and his untimely death deeply influenced his friend Eugène Delacroix.
