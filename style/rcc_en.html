What is the Rococo?

Major periods and regions
Early 18th century-late 18th century, French, etc.

Rococo
1715 The Accession of Louis XV
1789 The French Revolution
Boucher

Rococo is said to have flourished with the accession of Louis XV of France. Under Louis XIV, who favored grandeur, people grew weary of the rigid courtly world. Rococo emerged as a lighter, more ornate style in response. Yet glory never lasts forever. The age came to an end with the French Revolution. Like the Dream of Handan... prosperity is fleeting.

Point①
Bright, graceful expression born in reaction to Baroque

Watteau

Baroque art was grand and GOR-GEOUS! But... many works used dark colors and felt rather heavy. Rococo took a different path, using soft colors and delicate decoration to give paintings a lively rhythm. Flowing fabrics and graceful curves are also part of its charm! La la la~♪

Point②
More uninhibited works depicting romance and pleasure

Fragonard

Rococo became especially fond of scenes of romance between men and women. There was something for every taste, from idealized love to playful seduction, secret meetings, and even rather... suggestive scenes. Eek! I can't say any more than that~!

Point③
Its influence spread beyond France

Hogarth

Rococo was born in France, but its charm spread far beyond its borders! Believe it or not, I, Hogarth, a genius muse from Britain, am sometimes considered a representative of Rococo, too! In Italy, you can see Rococo influences in the works of Canaletto and Guardi. Got it~?

This is the Rococo piece!

Swing

This painting depicts a woman enjoying a swing, her husband pushing it, and her lover watching from the shade. Her flowing skirt and vivid colors exemplify Rococo style. Free from the gravity of Baroque art and religious instruction, the scene is filled with carefree pleasure. Its indulgent character was later criticized by Neoclassicists.
